Learn how real Korean sentences are built, one pattern at a time.

GRAMMAR

-고 싶어요

I want to...

When to use

Use this when you want to say that you want to do something.

Examples

한국에 가고 싶어요.

Hanguk-e gago sipeoyo.

I want to go to Korea.

한국 음식을 먹고 싶어요.

Hanguk eumsigeul meokgo sipeoyo.

I want to eat Korean food.

오늘 쉬고 싶어요.

Oneul swigo sipeoyo.

I want to rest today.

GRAMMAR

-아/어 주세요

Please...

When to use

Use this when politely asking someone to do something for you.

Examples

천천히 말해 주세요.

Cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo.

Please speak slowly.

물 좀 주세요.

Mul jom juseyo.

Please give me some water.

사진 찍어 주세요.

Sajin jjigeo juseyo.

Please take a picture.

GRAMMAR

-아/어도 돼요?

Can I...? / Is it okay if I...?

When to use

Use this when asking for permission.

Examples

여기 앉아도 돼요?

Yeogi anjado dwaeyo?

Can I sit here?

사진 찍어도 돼요?

Sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo?

Can I take a picture?

지금 먹어도 돼요?

Jigeum meogeodo dwaeyo?

Can I eat it now?

GRAMMAR

-아/어서

Because / So

When to use

Use this to connect a reason with its result.

Examples

피곤해서 일찍 잤어요.

Pigonhaeseo iljjik jasseoyo.

I was tired, so I went to bed early.

비가 와서 집에 있었어요.

Biga waseo jibe isseosseoyo.

It rained, so I stayed home.

맛있어서 많이 먹었어요.

Masisseoseo mani meogeosseoyo.

It was delicious, so I ate a lot.

GRAMMAR

-(으)ㄹ 거예요

Will / Going to

When to use

Use this to talk about a future plan or intention.

Examples

주말에 친구를 만날 거예요.

Jumare chingureul mannal geoyeyo.

I'm going to meet a friend this weekend.

내일 한국어를 공부할 거예요.

Naeil hangugeoreul gongbuhal geoyeyo.

I'm going to study Korean tomorrow.

저녁에 운동할 거예요.

Jeonyeoge undonghal geoyeyo.

I'm going to exercise this evening.

GRAMMAR

-아/어 봤어요?

Have you tried...? / Have you ever...?

When to use

Use this to ask whether someone has tried or experienced something.

Examples

한국 음식 먹어 봤어요?

Hanguk eumsik meogeo bwasseoyo?

Have you tried Korean food?

한복 입어 봤어요?

Hanbok ibeo bwasseoyo?

Have you tried wearing hanbok?

제주도 가 봤어요?

Jejudo ga bwasseoyo?

Have you been to Jeju Island?

One Word, Many Meanings

먹다: One Korean Verb, Many Meanings

먹다

🍚밥을 먹다

babeul meokda

eat a meal

Meaning

The basic meaning: eating food.

Example

점심 먹었어요?

Did you have lunch?

먹다

💊약을 먹다

yageul meokda

take medicine

Meaning

Koreans “eat” medicine, even liquid medicine.

Example

하루에 세 번 약을 먹어요

I take medicine three times a day

먹다

🎂나이를 먹다

naireul meokda

get older

Meaning

Every birthday, you “eat” one more year.

Example

한 살 더 먹었어요

I turned one year older

먹다

❤️마음을 먹다

maeumeul meokda

make up your mind

Meaning

“Eating” your heart means deciding firmly.

Example

한국어 공부하기로 마음먹었어요

I made up my mind to study Korean

Wear: One English Word, 6 Korean Verbs

WEAR VERB

옷을 입다

Oseul ipda

to wear clothes

👕입다

ipda

to wear (clothes)

Examples

티셔츠를 입어요.

Tisyeocheureul ibeoyo.

I'm wearing a T-shirt.

오늘 뭐 입을까요?

Oneul mwo ibeulkkayo?

What should I wear today?

WEAR VERB

신발을 신다

Sinbareul sinda

to wear shoes

👟신다

sinda

to wear (shoes, socks)

Examples

신발을 신어요.

Sinbareul sineoyo.

I put on my shoes.

운동화를 신고 나갔어요.

Undonghwareul singo nagasseoyo.

I put on my sneakers and went out.

WEAR VERB

모자를 쓰다 / 안경을 쓰다

Mojareul sseuda / Angyeong-eul sseuda

to wear a hat / glasses

🧢쓰다

sseuda

to wear (hats, glasses)

Examples

모자를 써요.

Mojareul sseoyo.

I wear a hat.

안경을 쓰고 책을 읽어요.

Angyeongeul sseugo chaegeul ilgeoyo.

I put on my glasses and read a book.

WEAR VERB

반지를 끼다

Banjireul kkida

to wear a ring

💍끼다

kkida

to wear (rings, gloves)

Examples

반지를 껴요.

Banjireul kkyeoyo.

I wear a ring.

겨울엔 장갑을 껴요.

Gyeouren janggabeul kkyeoyo.

I wear gloves in winter.

WEAR VERB

시계를 차다

Sigyereul chada

to wear a watch

⌚차다

chada

to wear (a watch)

Examples

시계를 차요.

Sigyereul chayo.

I wear a watch.

새 시계를 찼어요.

Sae sigyereul chasseoyo.

I put on a new watch.

WEAR VERB

가방을 메다

Gabang-eul meda

to wear a bag

🎒메다

meda

to wear (a bag on your shoulder or back)

Examples

가방을 메요.

Gabangeul meyo.

I carry a bag on my shoulder.

배낭을 메고 여행해요.

Baenangeul mego yeohaenghaeyo.

I travel with a backpack on.
